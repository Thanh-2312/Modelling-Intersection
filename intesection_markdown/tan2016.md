# Genetic Algorithm based Signal Optimizer for Oversaturated Urban Signalized Intersection

#### Abstract

Relieving traffic congestion is an urgent call for traffic engineering. Although various adaptive control strategies have been reported in literature to reduce the travel delay, most of them are not tested under oversaturated condition, where the traffic demand is higher than the road capacity. Therefore, this work proposes genetic algorithm (GA) to optimize the traffic signals for reducing the average delay at the at-grade crossed intersection under oversaturated condition. A comprehensive traffic model has been developed as the testbed. The average delay experienced by every vehicle to traverse the intersection is taken as performance metric to evaluate the performances of the formulated GA. The simulation results show the formulated GA is able to optimize the traffic signals and minimize the average delay of the intersection to 55.2 sec or equivalent to level-of-service (LOS) D.


Keywords: Genetic algorithm; traffic signal optimization; oversaturated traffic condition.

## 1. Introduction

Traffic congestion is major issue in urban areas due to rapid urbanization and motorization. Signal optimization have been proven as a cost effective strategy to relieve congestion [1]. In Malaysia, pretimed control is the common methodology to adjust the split based on the predetermined traffic demand. As such, it limits the ability of the controller to handle variations in traffic from the predetermined data. Therefore, the traffic signal control requires heuristic method for improving its adaptability to the dynamic changes in traffic flow.

Various adaptive control strategies have been reported in literature, such as reinforcement learning [2,3], fuzzy logic [4], agent based controller [5], artificial neural network [6], and genetic algorithm [7] but they are suffered from high computing cost. A large number of training data that contain all the traffic dynamics are required for regulating the algorithm parameters. Besides, most of these advanced techniques did not tested to handle oversaturated traffic condition.

Hence, this paper aims to formulate genetic algorithm (GA) for minimizing average delay for an urban signalized intersection under oversaturated condition. A comprehensive traffic model is developed as the testbed. GA is formulated to optimize traffic signals for each phase. Average delay experienced by vehicles is implemented as performance metric to evaluate the performance of the formulated GA.

## 2. Formulation of Genetic Algorithm

Genetic algorithm (GA) is formulated to search the optimum green time for each traffic phase based on the traffic condition. In this work, the chromosome consists of four genes that carry the potential solutions (effective green time ratio) for each traffic phase. The chromosome is represented in real-number which has the value in between 0 and 1 , as shown in Fig. 3. The total of the effective green time ratio for all the four phases must be equal to 1.000.

![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/887cb39a-9ea0-4202-a949-14b70706acfa-1.jpg?height=274&width=706&top_left_y=2168&top_left_x=1081)
Fig. 1: Real-number chromosome representation for effective green time ratio.

Computing process of the formulated GA can be divided into three stages: fitness evaluation, selection and reproduction, as shown in Fig. 2. Throughout the whole process, the algorithm will retain the optimum solution while eliminating those unfit solutions.

![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/887cb39a-9ea0-4202-a949-14b70706acfa-2.jpg?height=987&width=715&top_left_y=535&top_left_x=244)
Fig. 2: Computing process of formulated genetic algorithm.

Equation (1) defines the fitness function for evaluating the fitness value of each chromosome. For those chromosomes that achieve the desired outcome (minimize the average delay) will receive higher fitness value.

$$
\text { fitness }=\frac{1}{(x-r e f)}
$$

where $x$ is the effective green time ratio and ref is the proportion of critical flow ratio per phase in the intersection.

In the selection stage, the fitter chromosomes will receive preferential attention to be selected into a mating pool. Rank selection technique is implemented for the selection process.

The reproduction process can be divided into two parts: crossover and mutation. During the crossover process, two chromosomes will be selected randomly from the mating pool as parents. In order to preserve some fit chromosomes for next generation, crossover will only occur with a predefined crossover probability. If the selected parents are not subjected to crossover, then they will remain unmodified in the next generation. Blending crossover technique is used during the crossover process.

The mutation process is trying to keep diversity in the population. It helps to search the unexplored region in the solution space for obtaining the global optimum solution. However, the solutions may not be able to converge if the mutation probability is high. Table 1 and 2 summarize the techniques used for selection and reproduction stages, and parameters of the formulated GA respectively.

| Operator | Technique |
| :--- | :--- |
| Chromosome Representation | Real number |

| Crossover Operator | Blending |

## 3. Simulation Platform

An at-grade crossed intersection is modeled in Matlab m-file based on the guidelines of Public Works Department Malaysia (JKR) [8]. Clearance and start-up lost time are taken into consideration while developing the model. Few assumptions have been made for developing the intersection model:

i) No parking lane at the intersection
ii) Located in non-central business district
iii) Terrain level
iv) Uniform traffic distribution across all lanes

The traffic flow simulation runs at the rate of 1 sec, whereas the formulated GA samples at the rate of 120 sec. The performance of GA is tested under oversaturated condition, where the traffic demand is higher than lane group capacity.

Table 3 summarizes the parameters of traffic demand, saturation flow and capacity for each lane group in the intersection. The saturated flow is defined as the total number of vehicles that can traverse the intersection without interruption, whereas the capacity is defined as maximum sustainable flow rate during the simulation under a given traffic signal condition. When the traffic demand is higher than the capacity, the vehicles start to queue up at the lane group and hence increase the delay. ## 4. Results and Discussion

Fig. 4 shows the simulation results at the critical lane groups for each traffic phase. From the results, it can be observed that all the critical lane groups have experienced oversaturated traffic condition. Therefore, the vehicles in queue are unable to be cleared for every cycle. Some vehicles needs to wait for the next cycle to traverse the intersection, especially for the Phase 1 and 2 as shown in Fig. 4(a) and 4(b) respectively.

![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/887cb39a-9ea0-4202-a949-14b70706acfa-3.jpg?height=1748&width=749&top_left_y=777&top_left_x=1071)
Fig. 4: Number of vehicles in queue at critical lane group.

Although Phase 3 and 4 experienced oversaturated traffic condition, the queue length in both of phases are not as high as the Phase 1 and 2, as shown in Fig. 4(c) and 4(d) respectively. Sometimes, the queue length in these phases can be cleared within the cycle. This is because the traffic demand and the capacity are not much different. Therefore, the formulated GA will compromise the interest of the Phase 3 and 4 for the benefit of Phase 1 and 2 by distributing the delay to the Phase 3 and 4. As the result, the intersection experienced a level-of-ervice (LOS) D with an average delay of 52.2 sec. Table 4 summarizes the performance of formulated GA based on average delay and LOS.

## 5. Conclusion

This work has developed an at-grade crossed traffic intersection model with consideration of clearance and start-up lost time. Genetic algorithm (GA) is formulated to optimize the green time allocation for each phase and its performances are tested under oversaturated condition. Simulation results show the formulated GA is able to allocate appropriate green time for each phase based on the traffic demand. As the result, the average delay experienced by the intersection under oversaturated condition can be reduced to 52.2 sec or equivalent to level-of-service (LOS) D.
