# The traffic lights timer optimization model for a frequented intersection in a small urban area in Slovakia 

Veronika Harantová ${ }^{\mathbf{a}}$, Kristína Ovary Bulková ${ }^{\mathbf{a}}$<br>${ }^{a}$ University of Zilina, Faculty of Operation and Economic of Transport and Communications, Univerzitná 8215/1, 01026 Žilina, Slovakia,


#### Abstract

This paper focuses on the optimization of traffic light timing at a signal-controlled intersection in Kysucké Nové Mesto, a small urban area in northern Slovakia. A mathematical model, based on the Poisson distribution of vehicle arrivals, was developed, and several traffic control scenarios were simulated using AIMSUN software. The analysis demonstrated the potential for a significant reduction in the vehicles waiting time in the selected intersection, thereby increasing the intersection's capacity. The appropriate results were obtained by minimizing the sum of the mean vehicle waiting time during the green light duration.

## 1. Introduction and literature review

The light control system plays a crucial role in Intelligent Transport Systems (ITS) in alleviating traffic congestion. The optimization and proper traffic signal setting at intersections can be the way to achieve time savings for waiting vehicles. For example, four basic parameters such as the cycle lengths, green signal duration, phase sequence and "yellow signal" were optimized in the following studies by Yang (2016, Howell (2006), Yang (2008), Geng (2012) to minimize the delay time, traffic jams or the queue length. Accordingly in studies Yun (2005), Park et al. (2009), the stochastic optimizing approach was applied also for other parameters: the minimal and maximal green light lengths, the maximal red lights length, the time of the amber light and the position of detectors. Mentioned studies reported: the reduction in the average delay time of $15 \%-18 \%$ compared to synchronized light control, the reduction of vehicle

[^0]waiting time of $34 \%$ and pedestrians standing time of $70 \%$ in comparison to the original proposal of the intersection plans, as well as the reduction in the travel times of 2\% - 4\%. In Toledo et al. (2020), the experiment with three traffic light intersections in Haifa (Israel) provided the reduction of the delay time of $35 \%$ compared to the plan implemented in the field. The experiment was based on the simulations of 27 different scenarios with an optimized signalization plan with the aim to minimize average delay time.

Osorio and Selvam (2017) proposed the methods for efficiently simulating models transfer with large-scale highresolution traffic simulation models for simulation-based optimization. Their method was based on the evaluation of the algorithm with a traffic signal control problem on a small network and a city network. The large computational effort associated with the need to run thousands of microscopic simulations is the main disadvantage of the optimizing method based on the simulation.

The mesoscopic model for simulating the traffic named CAPACITEL is able for the reduction of this computational effort by Wolput (2015). This stochastic model for optimizing the traffic regulation is aimed at lengths of green lights cycles of isolated intersections.

During the optimization of lighting control in Antwerp (Belgium), Wolput et al. (2016) were looking for the pattern expressing the optimal cycle length as a function of lost time and intersection intensity ratio (Webster's equisaturation method) for the reduction of the delay time in the traffic. The result was a reduction in passenger delay time by 6, 17 and 22\% and bus delay time by 16, 27 and 3 \%.

Zachariah et al. (2017) worked with the automated static system for traffic regulation in intersections with the intention to minimize traffic jams consequences: the longer delay time, the production the emissions etc. A significant improvement in the average waiting time of $65,35 \%$ was observed. Harahap et al. (2019) used program MATLAB for optimal traffic lights setting depending on waiting time reduction. First the average speed of vehicles entering the intersection then average waiting time were estimated. Based on selected parameters, the waiting time reduced from 130 seconds to 55 seconds. Another possible method called is the Fuzzy Inference System of Mamdani which consists of process to change several input variables into a fuzzy set of linguistic variables each with its own affiliated functions (Komsiyah (2021)). The length of the green lights turned on can be optimized in real time based on several parameters (Hartanti (2019)): the increasing the traffic queue, the amount of the vehicles in each queue, the amount of the incoming vehicles to the queue, the speed of the vehicles or width of the lanes. Reducing the number of vehicles is expected to prevent traffic congestion, especially at intersections.

In this paper, the traffic lights timing optimization at the controlled intersection in the small town Kysucké Nové Mesto is realized. The developed mathematical model was simulated in AIMSUN software, which brought several scenarios of the controlling the transport on this road section.

## 2. Materials and methods

The observed intersection for this research is placed in the urban area of the city Kysucké Nové Mesto, where also leads the European road no. E75 (Figure 1a) also as a freight transit. The number of the cars and trucks passing the selected road cause long queues and vehicle congestion.

The data used for this research were collected from a traffic survey of vehicle intensity on November 6, 2024 in all directions for a duration of 12 hours. The highest traffic load during the peak hour (14:00-15:00) was 2021 vehicles (Figure 1b). These data were used for the simulation (peak hour).

Fig. 2 The phase structure of the intersection

For creating the simulation, the TSS (Transport Simulation System) program AIMSUN was used. The software enables modeling different simulations of the traffic situations from one isolated bus lane to the entire urban area in two- or three-dimensional visualisation (TSS (2020)). The realistic model of observed intersection with current phases of the controlling the intersection is represented on the Figure 2 below.

The control cycle length was set at 70 seconds, the minimum length of the green lights time is estimated at 5 seconds and the yellow lights is estimated at 3 seconds.

The dynamic simulation lasted for an hour and started after all parameters and conditions were identified. The total number of needed simulations was enumerated according to formula in AF - cityplan (2013) for 83 repeating. The average of the collected data from simulation represented output values, which were subsequently compared with the current state on observed intersection. Evaluated variables were travel time, delay time, the length of the queue and the vehicle speed. The optimizing of the lights signal based on mathematical model was used in an intention to improve the monitored variables. Mathematical model consisted of three concepts. The results of signal plan optimization were implemented in the simulation and compared with the current state.

## 3. Mathematical model for traffic lights timing optimization

The process explained and used in Hollá (2011) for following calculation. The initial assumptions are formulated as follows: the occurrence of vehicles has a Poisson distribution and the probability distribution of the time until the next variable occurs is exponential with parameter $\lambda$. There was a need to derive relations for mean values of total vehicles waiting time for green lights turned on (1) and the total unused green light time (2).

$$
E(Z)=\frac{\lambda t^{2}}{2},
$$

where $Z$ - total vehicles waiting time for green lights turned on [in seconds], $t$ - duration of the red lights [in seconds], $\lambda$ - the intensity of the incoming vehicles at the intersection [-].

$$
E(V)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(1-e^{-\lambda t}\right),
$$

where $V$ - total unused green lights time [in seconds].
Mentioned relations were used for the optimizing the time, which vehicles spent by waiting to green lights turned on at the intersection. Subsequently, the optimal duration of the green light was sought in three concepts (Hollá (2011)):

I. the sum of the mean values of the waiting time for the green lights turned on $E(Z)$ was minimal,
II. the sum of the mean values of the waiting time $E(Z)$ was minimal and the sum of the mean values of the total unused green lights time $E(V)$ was minimal,
III. the maximal value from the mean vehicles waiting time for the green lights turned on $E(Z)$ and the sum of the mean values of the total unused green lights time $E(V)$ was minimal.

![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/c3f7c47b-872c-4f1f-9074-059888d67fb5-4.jpg?height=306&width=945&top_left_y=467&top_left_x=502)
Fig. 3. Simplified diagram of the intersection with parameter $\lambda$ and period $x$

The condition for the green light duration was calculated using the available tools in MS Excel program. There was necessary to avoid the creating traffic queues infront the intersection. Thus, the duration of the green lights had to be greater, at most equal to the average time needed for passing vehicles accumulated during the red lights duration through the intersection also with vehicles which arrived at the intersection during green lights duration. The average time was established as the multiplication of the mean value of incoming vehicles and the average time the vehicle needs to leave the intersection (vehicle equipment time). The mean value of vehicles was established as the multiplication of the parameter $\lambda$ (the intensity of the incoming vehicles at the intersection) and the time of red or green lights duration. The average vehicle equipment time was established as the ratio of the length of the vehicles (unit length as 6 meters) and average speed of incoming vehicles ( 11 m/s or 40 km/h) at the intersection. The length of the intersection controlling cycle was left at the current 70 seconds.

The intersection schema with marked parameter $\lambda$ and period of traffic lights $x$ is on the following Figure 3, as well as the default setting of green lights duration on each shoulder:

The intensity of the incoming vehicles at the intersection are presented in all three phases by the parameter $\lambda_{\mathrm{i}}$. The green lights duration is expressed by variable $Y$. Thus, there is a consideration that the sum of green lights in all three phases takes as long as the setting is currently. Also, there are 4 parameters $\lambda_{\mathrm{i}}$ for the direction in the phase 1 and for directions in phase 2 and phase 3 each correspond to its own one parameter $\lambda_{\mathrm{i}}$. The variable Y represents the duration of the green lights determined on 59 seconds, because the amber lights take 11 seconds. In general, the light duration is marked as $x_{i}$, where $i$ represents the direction. For the minimizing of variables, the direction in the phase 2 is expressed with equation $x_{3}=Y-x_{1}-x_{2}$. Mentioned phases (Figure 3) based on green lights duration are described as follows:

- for the phase 1 is the green lights duration $x_{1}$ for the vehicles directed from shoulder A or shoulder C (directions K2, K3, K5, K6);
- for the phase 3 is the green lights duration $x_{2}$ for the vehicles directed from shoulder B (direction K4);
- for the phase 2 is the green lights duration $x_{3}$ for the vehicles directed from shoulder D (direction K1).

The mathematical model for the optimizing green light duration considers concepts mentioned above (Hollá (2011)):

I. the sum of the mean values of the waiting time for the green lights turned on should be minimal:
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{Z} \mathbf{1})+\ldots+\boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{Z 6}) \rightarrow \boldsymbol{\operatorname { m i n }}: \frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(Y-x_{1}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\left(Y-x_{1}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\left(Y-x_{1}\right)^{2}+ \\
& \frac{\lambda_{4}}{2}\left(Y-x_{1}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{6}}{2}\left(Y-x_{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{5}}{2}\left(Y-\left(Y-x_{1}-x_{2}\right)\right)^{2} \rightarrow \min
\end{aligned}
$$

$$

& \left.e^{-\lambda_{6} x_{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{5}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda_{5}\left(Y-x_{1}-x_{2}\right)}\right) \rightarrow \min
\end{aligned}
$$

III. the maximal value from the mean vehicles waiting time for the green lights turned on and the sum of the mean values of the total unused green lights time should be minimal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \max \{\boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{Z} \mathbf{1}), \boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{Z} \mathbf{6}), \boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{V} \mathbf{1}), \boldsymbol{E}(\boldsymbol{V} \mathbf{6})\} \rightarrow \boldsymbol{\operatorname { m i n }}: \max \left\{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(Y-x_{1}\right)^{2}, \frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\left(Y-x_{1}\right)^{2},\right. \\

& \frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda_{2} x_{1}}\right), \frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda_{3} x_{1}}\right), \frac{1}{\lambda_{4}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda_{4} x_{1}}\right), \frac{1}{\lambda_{6}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda_{6} x_{2}}\right), \frac{1}{\lambda_{5}}(1- \\
& \left.\left.e^{-\lambda_{5}\left(Y-x_{1}-x_{2}\right)}\right)\right\} \rightarrow \min
\end{aligned}
$$

with following conditions (6):

$$
x_{1} \geq \max \left\{\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3}, \lambda_{4}\right\} Y \frac{6}{11} ; x_{2} \geq \max \left\{\lambda_{6}\right\} Y \frac{6}{11} ;\left(Y-x_{1}-x_{2}\right) \geq \max \left\{\lambda_{5}\right\} Y \frac{6}{11}

## 4. Results

The sum of the green lights duration was stated on 59 seconds and parameters $\lambda_{\mathrm{i}}$ were determined based on the belonging directions of the intersection. The conditions were established using the nonlinear programming in MS Excel. The optimization of the green lights duration considering the expressed concepts (I), (II), (III) was performed by Excel Solver (Table 1).

## 5. Conclusion

The aim of this study was the traffic lights timing optimization at the controlled intersection with the intention to increase traffic flow and reduce vehicle waiting times. Several scenarios of the traffic controlling simulated in the AIMSUN software with using the mathematical model based on the Poisson distribution of vehicle occurrence were analysed.

The analysis of several control scenarios, focusing on minimizing the mean vehicle waiting time, demonstrated the potential for significant reductions in waiting times and increased intersection capacity. While the first optimization concept, minimizing the sum of mean waiting times, yielded the highest overall vehicle speeds and results comparable to the current state, challenges remain, particularly for the K6 direction, suggesting an area for future research to further enhance traffic flow at this intersection. However, it is important to emphasize that the optimal settings of the intersection control system depend on the specifications at the selected intersection, such as traffic intensity, intersection geometry or other factors.
