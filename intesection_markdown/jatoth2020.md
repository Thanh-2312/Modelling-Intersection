#### Abstract

Signalized intersections are the interrupted traffic flow facility in a roadway network. The efficiency of traffic operations at the signalized intersection is expected to improve during inter-green intervals. To improve the quality of service at an intersection the traffic control devices are used widely over the world. The time intervals defined for traffic signal operation help to reduce severe traffic conflicts, delay, and improve driver's decision for making turns. The present study evaluates the performance of traffic operations under with and without signal countdown timer (SCT) conditions based on field data collected at two signalized intersections in Hyderabad city, India. The vehicles speeding behavior and drivers complying with traffic regulation are also analyzed under different countdown timer conditions. It has been found that the average approaching speed of vehicles and redlight violations are found to be changed due to changes in the signal countdown timer conditions. The average startup loss time calculated in the field was found to be decreased when SCT was inactive. A significant increase in the capacity of signalized intersections has been observed when SCT was active. The traffic performance measures such as average control delay and queue lengths as estimated on the approaches were influenced due to the presence of STCs. The study finds that the signal countdown timer is an effective device that can enhance the traffic safety and operational performance of a signalized intersection.


Keywords Signal countdown timer • Red light violation • Capacity • Approaching speed

## 1 Introduction

A driver approaching a signalized intersection at the beginning of the amber time needs to decide whether clear the intersection or halt safely before the stop line at an approach. The collision between the vehicles may occur when their decision conflicts at the end of the green time [1]. Such confusions may be resolved by displaying the remaining time for the maneuver with the help of signal countdown timers (SCT). SCTs are becoming more popular nowadays as it is found to be an effective device when used as a part of traffic control strategies and management. The installation of SCTs serves many purposes such as reduce fuel consumption and anxiety

[^0]during queuing, decrease collision rate, improve intersection capacity and level of service [2-4].

Signal countdown timers are used as a technical device and accessories that can improve road safety and traffic control efficiency. However, scientific studies on safety and traffic control measures with and without the presence of SCTs are being performed over the world that differs significantly with locations due to change in driver behavior. Therefore, present study is proposed to evaluate the traffic performance measures at signalized intersections with active and inactive states of countdown timers.

## 2 Literature Review

A review of studies has been performed on the evaluation of signalized intersection performance including the effect of signal countdown timers. Kidwai and Rehan [5] conducted a study in Malaysia at different signalized intersections functioning with and without signal countdown timers. They witnessed a countdown timer did not influence the saturation flow rate hence no significant change in the capacity was observed at signalized intersections. However, Red Light

Violations (RLVs) were found to be reduced by 50\% in this study. Lum and Halim [1] acknowledged the traffic signal countdown timer (TSCT) for reducing red-light running (RLR) behavior. This study was performed with selected signalized intersections in Singapore, where a 65\% reduction in RLR behavior was achieved in one and a half months after the installation of the TSCT. However, long term observation revealed that RLR frequencies reached to pre-installation levels after 6 months. Sharma et al. [4] examined the impact of the signal countdown timer on headway in Chennai, India. It was found that TSCT technology can effectively reduce startup lost time and clearance lost time at signalized intersections. Chuanyun et al. [6] investigated two intersections in China for examining the effectiveness of red signal countdown timer. It was found that such timers not only reduce the initial headways of discharging vehicles but minimize fluctuation in headways of accumulated vehicles as well. The capacity was found to be increased by 5\% to 10\% afterward installation of the GSCT, however, it showed no effect on the saturation threshold. Devalla et al. [7] conducted a study before-and-after installation of the countdown timer at signalized intersections in New Delhi, India. The study finds that there is no change in saturation flow rate due to the countdown timer display. However, startup lost time was estimated to be significantly less as compared to the condition when there was no countdown timer. Similar findings obtained by Raksuntorn [8] investigated the impact of countdown signals on the startup lost time at signalized intersections of Bangkok, Thailand. The author found that the countdown signals had no effect on the saturation headway of the vehicles, but changed startup lost time significantly. Tang et al. [9] examined the influence of GSCT and cycle length on the queue discharge at signalized intersections in China. They observed GSCT associated with long cycle times resulted in increased startup lost time, unsteady saturation flow rate and abridged clearance lost time. Liu et al. [10] conducted a study to analyze the effects of SCTs on queue discharge characteristics at signalized intersections in China under free left-turn. TSCT ominously affected the start-up loss time of vehicles which increased the intersection throughout. Paul and Ghosh [11] evaluated the performance of signalized intersection with and without installation of SCT in Delhi, India. RLV happenings and extreme violation time through early 10 s of the red interval are higher in absence of GSCT. Yanjie et al. [12] examined waiting time predisposed by perceived waiting time, station facilities, trip circumstances, and respondent characteristics. The outcomes of this study measured making a decent atmosphere for waiting and helping passengers to smartly use public transport. Lee et al. [13] developed red light cameras at urban intersections to avoid road traffic signal violations and to decrease vehicle crashes in Daejeon Metropolitan City, South Korea.

## 4 Field Data

Field data was collected at two signalized intersections namely Chengicherla crossroad junction and Suchitra junction in Hyderabad city. Both are four-legged intersections with two major and two minor road approaches. The signalized intersections selected for the study were installed with signal countdown timers at all the approaches. The traffic flow data collected at selected intersections during different days of a week covered peak and non-peak hours with active and inactive signal countdown timers. Initially, traffic flow data was collected during the inactive signal countdown timer conditions on both the junctions. Then, these junctions were observed for two weeks with actively working signal countdown timers. The schematic diagram of both intersections is shown in Fig. 2a and b respectively.

Field data for the study was collected using a videography method covering peak and non-peak hours on Thursday and Friday, during morning from 7 a.m. to 10 a.m. and evening from 3 p.m. to 6 p.m. Recorded video was played on a widescreen display and extracted traffic flow data at every 15 min intervals. The queue length was observed from video files in every cycle by counting the number of vehicles joining the queue at approaches of signalized intersections. The approaching speed data was also obtained using Radar guns at upstream locations about 70-80 m away from the stop line of intersections. The photograph of selected intersections is shown in Fig. 3. The composition of vehicle types observed at two intersections is shown in Fig. 4.

Approaching traffic volume was observed at both intersections from videos. At Suchitra junction, traffic volume is found in the range of 1200 veh/h to 2500 veh/h and in case of Chengicherla crossroad junction, it is observed between 900 veh/h to 2300 veh/h during whole observation periods.

## 5 Field Data Analysis

### 5.1 Red Light Violations (RLVs) Analysis

RLVs of vehicles were analyzed from observed field data at selected intersections with active and inactive countdown timer conditions. Synchronization between traffic signal timings and vehicle arrival was done at approaches during the start of red time and end of red time. It is observed that the values of average RLVs per cycle are found to be increased from 7 to 9 when the countdown timer was not functioning at Chengicherla crossroad intersection. In case of Suchitra junction, the values of average RLVs per cycle with timer active and inactive conditions are observed to be 4 and 5 respectively. The average RLVs per cycle were aggregated during 15 min time interval and the frequency of vehicle type two-wheelers is found to be more on both signalized intersections.

Usually, drivers do not aware of the duration of red interval under inactive SCTs. If a suitable gap is available within the conflicting traffic, they tend to make maneuvers at the beginning of red time. Similarly at the end of red time drivers are not aware that when green time is going to start so they simply wait in the queue. Therefore, RLVs tend to decrease under inactive countdown timer condition at end of red time. As the numbers of vehicles crossing the intersections at beginning of red time are more than the end of red time, so overall RLVs per cycle were found higher under inactive timer conditions. But in case of active timer condition, drivers are aware of the start and end of red time duration and therefore afford or manage to spend time at approach (by switching off their engine) could be the reason to have a reduction in red light violations in compared to inactive timer condition.

### 5.2 Approaching Speed Analysis

Approaching speed of vehicles was analyzed to examine whether there is any effect of signal countdown timer on approaching vehicles. It is found that the approaching speeds of vehicles are higher when countdown timers were active. The approaching speed of individual vehicle types was tested under timer active and inactive conditions for comparison. Z-test (sample size = 180) was performed to examine whether the average speed of vehicle types approaching the intersection is different under different signal countdown timer conditions. A significant increase in approaching speed has been observed in presence of active signal countdown timer as $p$ value (p value <0.0001) is estimated to be very less when tested at 5\% significant level. The comparison of approaching speeds of different vehicle types at approaches of intersections is shown in Fig. 7.

## 6 Conclusions

Following conclusions may be drawn based on the present study.

1. The average approaching speed of vehicles observed at selected intersections has shown a significant difference under timer active and inactive conditions. A significant increase in approaching speed has been observed during

## the green time when signal operations are functioning

 with SCTs.2. Number of red light violations (RLVs) is found to be decreased in presence of active countdown timer. This may result in reduction of prominent right-angle crashes and thereby improving intersection safety.
3. Present study confirms that there is a significant reduction in start-up lost time of discharging vehicles in presence of active countdown timer. It may be inferred that the vehicles waiting for maneuvering in front of the queue takes more time to start-up under inactive countdown timer condition. The time taken by traffic stream to achieve saturation flow state is found to be lower at intersection with active countdown timer than intersection with inactive countdown timer. Finally, reduction in startup lost time resulted in higher capacity.
4. Average control delay at signalized intersections is found to be decreased in presence of countdown timer which may result in an improvement in the level of service (LOS).

The findings from the present study recommend that the signal countdown timer helps to increase the operational performance of signalized intersections.
