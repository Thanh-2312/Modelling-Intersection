# Proactive safety evaluation of a multilane unsignalized intersection using surrogate measures 

S. Shekhar Babu ${ }^{\mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b}}$ and P. Vedagiri ${ }^{\mathrm{c}}$<br>${ }^{\mathrm{a}}$ Department of Civil Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, Mumbai, India; ${ }^{\mathrm{b}}$ Faculty of Civil Engineering, College of Military Engineering, Pune, India; ${ }^{\text {c }}$ Transportation Systems Engineering, Department of Civil Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, Mumbai, India


#### Abstract

Road traffic safety is emerging as an area of increased attention and concern in many countries including India. Safety evaluation by conflict study using surrogate measures is a proactive method which does not require any accident data. Conflict study may be carried out using post encroachment time (PET) as a surrogate measure, where critical conflicts are determined based on certain threshold value of PET. Identifying critical conflicts using a threshold value of PET is good and correct for highways and major roads where traffic follows posted speed, but on highways with mixed traffic for varied speeds this is not correct. It can be seen that conflicts with PET less than the threshold value may not be critical if the speed of conflicting vehicle is less. Similarly, conflicts with PET more than threshold value may also be critical if the speed of conflicting vehicle is high. The speed of conflicting vehicle is also important in evaluating severity of conflict. Hence, the use of PET alone for conflict study is not correct for evaluating safety of intersections on highways where traffic follows varied speeds. Therefore, in the present study, safety evaluation of an unsignalized intersection is carried out using two surrogate measures, PET and the speed of corresponding conflicting through vehicle. A term critical speed is proposed to identify critical conflicts. Critical speed for a particular PET value is determined using stopping/braking distance concept. Results show that there are significant percent of conflicts which are critical at the intersection. This shows that drivers of rightturning vehicles do take risks in accepting small gaps in through traffic at the intersection, which is dangerous. As crossing maneuver depends on length and speed of turning vehicle, the effect of type of turning vehicle has also been studied. Results show that right-turning light motor vehicles are at higher risk compared to two wheelers and heavy vehicles at the intersection. Safety evaluation of such unsignalized intersections may be carried out using this concept.

## Introduction

Rapid economic development of cities in India has generated correspondingly rapid growth in travel demand. Expansion of cities into suburbs and non-availability of adequate, efficient and economical public transportation services around Indian cities have made people to use their own vehicles to move around. At the same time, rising income levels among Indian middle-class population have made car and motorcycle ownerships affordable. As per 2011 statistical data, vehicle population of India is more than 140 million and further increasing at a rate more than 10\% per annum. Traffic risk and exposure has increased due to exponential growth of vehicle population. During the year 2011, around 0.5 million road accidents have occurred in India, which killed about 142,000 people and injured more than half a million persons, many of whom are disabled for rest of their lives. These numbers translate into one road accident every minute, and one road accident death in less than four minutes. (Source: Government of India, Ministry of road transport and highways, transport research wing, New Delhi.) More than 50\% of all urban road accidents occur at intersections because of unsafe driver actions and maneuvers. Many new road intersections are being created due to increased urban development where major roads such as arterial/ sub-arterials and highways are intersected by several cross streets or minor roads. Most of these intersections are unsignalized intersections where vehicles attempt crossing and merging with through traffic on major roads. These maneuvers are highly complex and dangerous and have direct influence on safety of road users. Moreover, traffic on Indian roads is highly heterogeneous comprising
a wide range of vehicles with varying static and dynamic characteristics which further aggravate the problem.

Traffic safety is emerging as an area of increased attention and concern in many countries including India. Several countermeasures are being practiced across the world to improve traffic safety. Researchers have suggested many predictive methods to evaluate the impact of these measures on safety. Traditional methods of traffic safety evaluation are based on past accident data where safety is measured in terms of number of traffic accidents using different statistical approaches. This accident data approach is regarded as reactive approach, implying that a significant number of accidents must be recorded to evaluate a particular traffic safety measure. Other shortcomings of this approach are low quality and unavailability of accident data. It is observed that many accidents which do not involve any injury are generally not reported due to obvious reasons. Hence, relying on accident reports may sometimes give biased conclusions (Chin and Quek 1997). Though road accident count is significantly high, accident frequencies segregated by locations, time, and type are generally very low. It is difficult to derive statistical inferences by merely examining accident counts with this low rate of occurrence. Moreover, these methods require longer time period to statistically validate safety measures. Hence, there is a distinct need for safety evaluation method which is faster, more resource effective, reliable, and does not require any accident data.

Safety evaluation by conflict study using surrogate measures is a better method to overcome all the above stated problems. Conflict study is focused on observing serious conflicts or near miss accidents. The idea of traffic conflict has been employed by transportation engineers in identifying hazardous locations on highways in the past also. Traffic conflicts are defined as incidents which do not necessarily involve accidents. Traffic conflict technique has the ability to identify the occurrence of near miss accidents directly in real-time traffic and therefore offers a faster and more representative way of estimating expected accident frequency and accident outcomes (Archer 2000). Hence traffic conflict study is a proactive method of safety evaluation which does not require any accident data. Moreover, the conflict data can be collected within a shorter time period using video cameras, thus require less time for safety evaluation. Further, these methods could also be used for evaluating safety of roadway design which is yet to be built or any traffic control strategy yet to be implemented, using simulation.

## Literature review

Traffic conflict studies have been carried out in the past by several researchers to identify hazardous traffic situations by observing instances when drivers took evasive actions to avoid being involved in collisions like sudden changing of lanes, hard braking, etc. Some conflict studies have made use of quantitative measurements in terms of time and space proximity between vehicles using video technique. However, for effective implementation of this technique, it is necessary that the conflicts are well-defined and are determined accurately. A unified definition was proposed at the first workshop on traffic conflict to provide some consistency in conflict definition (Chin and Quek 1997). Conflict is defined as 'An observable situation in which two or more road users approach each other in time and space to such an extent that there is risk of collision if their movements remain unchanged' (Gettman and Head 2008). Accepting this general definition of conflicts, researchers have projected conflicts as potential collisions of low degree of danger, pre incidents of collision, and near miss accidents. Relationship between conflicts and accidents is well-described by safety pyramid model (Hydén 1987), which illustrates that all traffic events contain some kind of interaction between road users with varying degrees of severity. Conflicts close to the tip of pyramid are with low frequency but of high degree of severity, hence represented as near miss accidents. This also shows that conflicts close to the base of pyramid are with low degree of severity and form the major proportion of observed interactions. Thus, the frequency of occurrence of conflicts decreases with increasing severity level.

Safety analysis by conflict study is to identify serious conflicts or near miss crashes which are termed as critical conflicts. There are many proximal safety indicators like time to collision (TTC), post encroachment time (PET), and deceleration rate (DR) which are defined as measures of crash proximity, based on temporal and spatial measures that reflect the closeness of road users, in relation to a projected point of collision. Previous research studies have shown that there is good correlation between accident rates and conflicts.

PET is the most suitable and popular proximal safety indicator for determining crossing conflicts (Klunder, Abdoelbasier, and Immers 2004). Conflicts with low values of PET are potential accidents as there might not be enough time available to the driver of conflicting vehicle to respond and take evasive actions such as braking or changing lanes, to avoid collision with the offending vehicle. Critical conflicts are judged based on certain value of PET which is termed as threshold values of PET. However, it is difficult to determine how short the threshold value of PET should be to identify a critical conflict. It is seen that different drivers have different response times and they might also undertake different evasive actions depending upon vehicle's performance and prevailing traffic conditions (Sultan and McDonald 2003). PET threshold
values used by researchers as 1 s (Archer 2000) and as 5 s (Caliendo and Guida 2012) where critical conflicts are determined if PET values are less than the threshold value. Conflicts with PET values less than 1 s are generally unsafe (Archer 2000; Klunder, Abdoelbasier, and Immers 2004). Conflict studies have also been carried out using simulation and the simulated conflicts are compared with actual crash data where good correlation between conflicts and accidents have been obtained (Caliendo and Guida 2012; Dijkstra et al. 2010; Ozbay et al. 2007).

Ozbay et al. (2007) in their study proposed a safety indicator as modified time to collision (MTTC). They have identified a road stretch with high accident records with details of type, location, time, etc. For the same stretch, they collected traffic volume and vehicular composition and calculated MTTC value for the traffic data by simulation. The threshold value for MTTC was taken as 4 s to identify critical conflicts. Simulation results were validated by comparing accident data which show good correlation.

Surrogate Safety Assessment Model (SSAM) software is developed to automate the process of identifying conflicts by calculating surrogate safety measures (FHWA 2008). This software works with the data of trajectories of vehicles driving through a traffic facility and determining the conflicts. The software generates conflicts based on two surrogate safety measures TTC and PET corresponding to vehicle to vehicle interaction. The software takes the default threshold values for TTC and PET as 1.5 and 5 s, respectively. Caliendo and Guida (2012) in their study used SSAM software for determining conflicts at unsignalized intersections. Critical conflicts have been identified taking the default threshold values and compared with the actual crash data of the intersections. A good correlation between recorded crashes and critical conflicts computed by SSAM was observed.

Peesapati et al. (2011) in their study developed a semi- automated data collection methodology that more readily allows for in-field measurement of profile-based surrogate measures, such as, speed and DR. For extracting useful data from the video, video processing software was developed which can also be used for video obtained from small angles along a longer stretch of approach road. Post-processing and analysis of the video produces time and position data for each vehicle. Speed and acceleration/ deceleration profiles of vehicles may then be calculated from the developed time-space profiles accurately.

Pirdavani et al. (2010) in their study introduced simulation-based method of traffic safety evaluation of unsignalized intersections under different traffic conditions. They used micro simulator to observe PET values for different traffic volumes and varying speeds on both roadways of the intersection. With an increase in the volume of major roads the probability of finding large gaps in through traffic becomes smaller and consequently, crossing vehicles from minor roads start accepting smaller gaps, thus low values of PET are recorded. Results show that with the increase in traffic volumes and posted speeds PET values decrease and worsen the safety of the intersection.

Alhajyaseen(2015) in his study proposed a measurement of crash hazard that considers crash occurrence probability as well as expected severity. A term conflict Index is proposed which is derived by utilizing the change in total kinetic energy before and after the collision, angle of collision, and PET. The estimated conflict characteristics are compared with the records of severe crashes. It is found that the proposed safety measure is successful in similarly ranking different signalized intersection to the severity of crashes that occurred at each site.

Several researchers have carried out conflict study using PET as surrogate measure and critical conflicts are determined by taking a threshold value of PET. Identifying critical conflicts using specified threshold value of PET is correct if major road traffic follows the stipulated posted speed. If the traffic is heterogeneous and moving with varied speeds then identifying critical conflicts based on a particular threshold value of PET is not correct. Two conflicts with a particular PET value may have different speeds of conflicting vehicle and the conflict with high conflicting speed is more dangerous than the conflict with less conflicting speed. It can be observed that conflicts with PET more than threshold value may also be critical if speed of conflicting vehicle is high. Similarly, the conflict with PET less than threshold value may not be critical if speed of conflicting vehicle is low. Thus, observing conflicts using PET alone is not correct. The speed of conflicting vehicle approaching towards the crossing vehicle is also an important parameter reflecting the severity of conflicts. If the speed of conflicting vehicle is high then it is difficult to stop this vehicle in order to avoid collision if such situation arises. Previous studies show that PET alone cannot assess the safety of angle collisions. The vehicle speed during a crash (crash speed) significantly contributes to the severity of that crash (Alhajyaseen 2015). Therefore, in the present study, the conflicts are observed by determining PET values along with speeds of corresponding conflicting through vehicles. Thus, every conflict is defined by two surrogates; its PET value and the speed of conflicting vehicle and critical conflicts are identified based on both the parameters. For a particular PET value, a specific speed value is determined using braking distance concept. This speed is termed as 'critical speed' which is used to identify critical conflicts.

## Study objective and methodology

In the present study, safety evaluation of a typical threearm unsignalized intersection on a multilane road has
been carried out using surrogate measures. The intersection under study has two arms along the major road and third arm joins a minor road. Traffic rule in India is keep left; hence left-turning vehicles both from major road and from minor road at the intersection do not face much difficulty, but the right-turning vehicles from major road going towards minor road and from minor road going towards major road have to stop and wait at the intersection in order to cross through facing traffic of major road. Drivers of right-turning vehicles have to find gaps in facing through traffic on all lanes of major road. On getting acceptable gaps on all the lanes, drivers of right-turning vehicles start crossing through traffic, while conflicting through vehicles approach towards them. If major road has high traffic volume, then drivers have to wait longer in order to find acceptable gaps. Further due to long waiting time, the drivers of waiting vehicles lose patience and thus, may accept small gaps which is dangerous and unsafe (Pirdavani et al. 2010). Objective of the study is to evaluate safety of the intersection by observing the crossing conflicts using surrogate safety measures.

In this study, conflicts are observed using two surrogate measures, PET and speed of conflicting vehicle. PET is defined as the time lag between the passages of offending vehicle and conflicting vehicle on a conflict zone i.e. the potential conflict area. For an intersection, the offending vehicle is the right-turning vehicle (keep left rule in India) and the conflicting vehicle is the through vehicle on major road. Every right-turning vehicle may have conflicts on all the lanes on the major road. Crossing conflicts due to right-turning vehicles with facing through conflicting vehicles on a typical unsignalized intersection for major road and for minor road are shown in Figures 1 and 2, respectively.

As the right-turning vehicle starts crossing the through traffic on major road, the driver of conflicting through vehicle approaching the conflict area also observes this maneuver and thus reacts accordingly. Some drivers reduce their speed to allow the crossing vehicle to clear the conflict area but few drivers may approach the conflict area without reducing speed presuming that the crossing vehicle will clear the conflict area before their arrival. The later action may be dangerous if the crossing vehicle does not clear the conflict area. This may happen if the driver of turning vehicle could not find gaps in through traffic from other direction in order to merge with it and the median width at the intersection is not adequate to shadow turning vehicle. In such situations, the driver of conflicting through vehicle has to stop the vehicle to avoid collision with the crossing vehicle as it may not be feasible to suddenly change the lane on a multi lane road. It is observed that drivers are more likely to brake than to steer in hazardous conditions.

For a conflict with certain PET value and speed of conflicting vehicle, when turning vehicle just leaves the conflict area, the conflicting through vehicle is positioned at a distance equal to PET times the speed of conflicting vehicle (PET × speed of conflicting vehicle) from the conflict area. If this distance is more than the required stopping distance for that speed of conflicting vehicle, then the conflict is not critical. If the distance is less than the required stopping distance, it is a critical conflict. Therefore, to separate critical and non critical conflicts, the available distance is equated with the required stopping distance and a speed value is determined for that PET value which can be used to identify critical conflicts. This speed of conflicting vehicle is termed as critical speed for that PET value.

As drivers of conflicting vehicles have already reacted to the crossing maneuver, the perception distance is not considered. The stopping or braking distance ' $d$ ' is calculated as $v^{2} / 2 g f$ where $v$ is the speed of conflicting vehicle (m/s), $g$ is gravitational acceleration $\left(\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}\right)$, and $f$ is coefficient of friction between tyre and road surface. Equating the available distance with braking distance, critical speed for that PET value can be determined as PET $\times 2$ gf. Critical speeds for different PET values are determined using this concept by taking g as $9.81 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$ and coefficient of friction as 0.35 (Lamm, Psarianos, and Mailaender 1999). ## Data collection

The intersection under study is a typical three-arm unsignalized intersection situated in Thane city, in Mumbai. The intersection has two arms along major road and third arm joining a minor road. The major road is a six-lane divided carriageway connecting Thane city to Vashi, whereas the minor road is a four-lane divided carriageway connecting Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation (MIDC) area of Thane city. Figure 3 shows a snapshot of MIDC Thane intersection. Traffic at the intersection consists of trucks, buses, cars, two wheelers (TW), and auto rickshaws. The intersection has straight approaches and is free from the influence of bus stops, parking, etc. It is observed that the traffic on major road going towards Thane is mainly moving on the right-most two lanes, whereas the traffic going towards Vashi is moving on the left-most two lanes. As traffic rule in India is keep left, these maneuvers do not pose any major threat to the safety, but right-turning traffic on major road from Thane going towards minor road has to cross through traffic on the major road. Similarly right-turning traffic from minor road going towards Vashi, has to cross through traffic on major road. Hence, these movements do pose threat to the safety at the intersection. Further the right-turning traffic from the minor road has to merge with the through traffic on the major road going towards Vashi. Through traffic on the major road going towards Vashi is mainly moving on the left-most two lanes and the right most lane at the intersection is normally occupied by the right-turning vehicles from Thane going towards minor road waiting for acceptable gaps as the width of the median is only 1.2 m. Hence, these waiting vehicles shadow the merging vehicles, thus not posing much problem with the merging maneuvers. Therefore, only crossing conflicts due to right-turning vehicles both from minor road and major road with facing through traffic on the major road are considered for analysis.

Conflict area of the intersection is along the major road on the right-most two lanes towards minor road. Video camera is positioned such that the full view of the conflict area is clearly visible. Conflicts due to crossing maneuver were observed by determining PET and speeds of conflicting through vehicles. PET is determined as time gap between the crossing vehicles leaving a particular conflict area and the through vehicle entering the same conflict area. Speed of corresponding conflicting through vehicle at the intersection is also measured. PET value of a conflict depends on several factors like speed, acceleration, and length of right-turning vehicle, and also on the speed of conflicting through vehicle besides the driver's behavior in accepting the gaps. Therefore, in the present study, the type of turning vehicle also is considered for determining conflicts. All right-turning vehicles are separated in three categories, as TW, light motor vehicles (LMV), and heavy vehicle (HV). LMV includes cars, auto-rickshaw and mini vans, whereas HV includes buses and trucks. Type of through vehicle is not considered as the length of through vehicle does not affect PET value.

Traffic flow data of the intersection has been collected using video camera for about one hour. Conflict area is divided in conflict zones, each measuring 3.5 m × 3.5 m square grids as shown in Figure 4. Grids are made on AutoCAD and then overlaid on video film using Corel video Studio Pro X6 software. Recorded video film is played at a speed of 25 frames per second using AVS video Editor Software. Every click of mouse steps up by 0.040 s. Thus, accuracy of PET measurement is 0.040 s. Video is run frame by frame and PET values are calculated by noting down two time events $t_{1}$ and $t_{2}$. ' $t_{1}$ ' is the time event at which right-turning vehicle exits conflict zone, i.e. rear of turning vehicle exactly leaves the conflict grid. The time can be read from timer in the software which gives time in minutes and seconds. ' $t_{2}$ 'is the time event at which through vehicle on major road exactly enters the respective conflict grid, i.e. the front of the vehicle touches the conflict grid. Difference between $t_{2}$ and $t_{1}$ is measured as PET. Speeds of conflicting through vehicles approaching the conflict zone are determined along with their PET values. For calculation of speed, the time taken by conflicting through vehicle for traversing the distance over the grids is noted and the speed is determined.

## Conclusions

Safety evaluation of unsignalized intersections using PET is good for highways where traffic follows posted speed of highways. Critical conflicts are determined taking a threshold value of PET. But for highways with mixed traffic with varied speeds safety evaluation by PET alone is not correct. Therefore, in the present study, conflicts are observed using two surrogates, PET and speed of corresponding conflicting through vehicle. A term critical speed is proposed to identify critical conflicts. Critical speed for a particular PET value is determined based on braking distance concept. It is found that there are significant percent of observed conflicts which are critical at the intersection. This shows that drivers of right-turning vehicles do take risks and accept small gaps in through traffic which is dangerous and unsafe. Considering type of right-turning vehicle, it is observed that the right-turning LMVs are at higher risk as compared to HV and TW. This may be due to the size of the turning vehicle. When a HV is turning, conflicting through vehicles decrease their speeds; hence low percent of critical conflicts are recorded for HV conflicts. When TW is turning, it clears the conflict area faster than LMV, thus recording high PET values, hence resulting low percent of critical conflicts.

The intersection under study has speeds of conflicting through vehicles up to 52 kmph only, therefore five groups of critical conflicting speed have been used. There may be other multi lane major roads where conflicting vehicles may approach intersection up to 100 kmph. In that case, few more critical speed groups are required to get critical conflicts. Thus, this methodology will be useful to evaluate safety of unsignalized intersections for highways with mixed traffic. If the intersection has higher percent of critical conflicts, then the intersection is unsafe and further remedial measures could be taken accordingly. Safety evaluation of different intersections for varying traffic volume and turning traffic may also be carried out by simulation using this concept. The influence of type of through vehicle may also be considered for further research.
